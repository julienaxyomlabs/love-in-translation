# 2. Definitions and conceptual distinctions

## Love is a family of constructs, not one quantity

Science does not treat "love" as one construct. Lay concepts of love are organized around prototypes with fuzzy borders: maternal love, romantic love, affection and infatuation are better or poorer examples of the category, not instances of one definition ([Fehr & Russell 1991](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.60.3.425)) [Established for the lay concept]. Ellen Berscheid called the failure to explicate the love construct "the major obstacle" to its study. She proposed tracking several distinct types of love, each with its own causes and time course ([Berscheid 2010](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.093008.100318)) [Theoretical]. Sternberg's triangular theory separates intimacy, passion and decision/commitment, and distinguishes the *amount* of love (the absolute strength of the components) from its *kind* (their relative strength) ([Sternberg 1986](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.93.2.119)) [Theoretical]. The three-component measurement model holds across 25 countries, with configural, metric and scalar invariance in 7,332 respondents ([Sorokowski et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1080/00224499.2020.1787318)) [Established for the measurement model]. A meta-analytic factor analysis of 81 studies (N = 19,387) found a general love factor alongside separable "romantic obsession" and "practical friendship" factors ([Graham 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407510389126)) [Moderate/mixed]. Compassionate love, the motivation to promote another's welfare, has its own scale and its own correlates, including providing support ([Sprecher & Fehr 2005](https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407505056439)) [Moderate/mixed].

This has a methodological consequence that recurs throughout the paper. In self-report, love does not separate cleanly from relationship evaluation. In the Perceived Relationship Quality Components inventory, satisfaction, commitment, intimacy, trust, passion and love load on **one higher-order factor** ([Fletcher, Simpson & Thomas 2000](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167200265007)) [Established]. A bifactor analysis of items from 34 relationship measures (N = 3,439) found one general sentiment factor accounting for most of their shared variance ([Kim et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0342451)) [Moderate/mixed]. A study that uses self-reported love as the "amount" and self-reported satisfaction as the outcome is therefore close to tautological. "Amount of love" has to be operationalized separately: by component, from the giver's side, or through behaviour.

The working definition used here is deliberately plural. "Love" refers to a set of latent states in a person: felt affection and attraction, a motivation to promote the other's welfare, commitment to the relationship, and an attachment bond. "Care" refers to observable acts directed at the other's welfare. The paper never needs a single number for love; it needs only to know which component a claim concerns.

## Ten foundational questions, answered

| Question from the [brief](../docs/research-brief.md) | Short answer | Basis | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Is love scientifically definable as one construct? | No. It is a family of partly independent states (passion, intimacy or companionship, commitment, compassionate motivation, attachment). | [Fehr & Russell 1991](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.60.3.425); [Graham 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407510389126); [Berscheid 2010](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.093008.100318) | Established (structure) |
| Are intentions observable? | Only inferable. Even "overperception" of intent cannot be separated from targets understating their intentions, because true intentions are never directly measured. | [Perilloux & Kurzban 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614555727) | Moderate/mixed |
| Is love expressed always recognizable as love? | No. Unambiguous support is read similarly by everyone, but ambiguous messages diverge by the receiver's attachment, and about half of sacrifices are missed. | [Collins & Feeney 2004](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.87.3.363); [Visserman et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550618757599) | Moderate/mixed |
| Can someone perceive love without receiving appropriate care? | Yes. Perceived "false-alarm" sacrifices still produce gratitude, idealization predicts satisfaction, and in aggressive relationships committed partners re-label aggression as tolerable or playful. | [Visserman et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550618757599); [Murray, Holmes & Griffin 1996](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.70.1.79); [Arriaga 2002](https://doi.org/10.1177/0886260502017006001) | Moderate/mixed |
| Can someone receive meaningful care without perceiving loving intentions? | Yes. Unnoticed support improves adjustment and goal attainment, and household income improves children's behavioural and educational outcomes whether or not it is read as love. | [Bolger et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.6.953); [Girme, Overall & Simpson 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167213497802); [Cooper & Stewart 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12187-020-09782-0) | Moderate/mixed |
| Is need fulfilment equivalent to being loved? | No. Needs can be met by people who do not love us (relational regulation theory) or met strategically, and love can coexist with unmet needs. | [Lakey & Orehek 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023477); [Drigotas & Rusbult 1992](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.62.1.62) | Theoretical / Moderate |
| Does making someone feel understood imply compatibility? | No. Goal conflict harms quality and well-being independently of understanding. Interdependence theory treats conflict of interests as structural, not informational. | [Gere & Schimmack 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10902-011-9314-2); [Rusbult & Van Lange 2003](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.54.101601.145059) | Moderate/mixed |
| Can high responsiveness coexist with manipulation? | Plausibly yes, but not directly tested for perceived partner responsiveness. Exploitative narcissism is most attractive at first acquaintance, emotion-regulation knowledge amplifies Machiavellian deviance, and affection made conditional on compliance functions as control. | [Back, Schmukle & Egloff 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016338); [Côté et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611416251); [Kanat-Maymon et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/jopy.12171) | Preliminary (for intimate relationships) |
| Can relationship stability exist without love? | Yes. Moral and structural commitment, lack of alternatives and institutional commitment sustain "stable unhappy" marriages, at a well-being cost. | [Johnson, Caughlin & Huston 1999](https://doi.org/10.2307/353891); [Heaton & Albrecht 1991](https://doi.org/10.2307/352748); [Hawkins & Booth 2005](https://doi.org/10.1353/sof.2005.0103) | Moderate/mixed |
| Can love exist without healthy relationship functioning? | Yes. Love and commitment coexist with abuse, and sincere parental love coexists with psychological control, which is linked to ill-being across cultures. | [Kearney 2001](https://doi.org/10.1002/nur.1029); [Bradshaw et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0001389) | Moderate / Established |

## Constructs that must remain separate

The brief asks which concepts must not be merged. The answer, defended in later sections, is the fourteen constructs below. Each has a distinct measurement route and distinct empirical correlates, and each has been shown, or can be shown, to vary independently of at least one neighbour. Collapsing them into an overall relationship score would hide three things the evidence shows to matter:
- **trade-offs**, such as achievement-oriented control that co-occurs with higher grades *and* more depressive symptoms ([Zhang et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-023-01845-z));
- **costs borne by the giver** that dyad-level satisfaction masks ([Le et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000133));
- **danger signals** that run in the opposite direction from love ratings ([Arriaga, Capezza & Daly 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000035)) [Moderate/mixed].

| Construct | Working definition | What it is not | Why it must stay separate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Love (components) | Latent felt affection, welfare motivation, commitment, attachment | Not behaviour; not satisfaction | Predicts outcomes directly; self-report overlaps satisfaction |
| Intention | A's plan to benefit B in some way at some time | Not the act | Latent; diverges from enactment under constraint |
| Capacity | The set of acts A can actually perform (time, money, energy, skill, stress) | Not motivation | Explains "loving but absent" without low love |
| Enacted care (expression) | Acts A performs for B, by channel | Not B's report of them | Measurable by A's log or by observers |
| Signal / visibility | What of the act, and of its cost, is observable to B | Not the act itself | Provision and sacrifice often have invisible costs |
| Detection | Whether B registers that the act occurred | Not its meaning | Quasi-signal-detection data show misses and false alarms |
| Construal and motive inference | What B believes the act means and why A did it | Not detection | Perceived motive determines gratitude |
| Perceived regard or responsiveness | B's sense of being understood, valued and cared for | Not need fulfilment | Partly projected; predicts outcomes strongly |
| Need fulfilment (received care) | Degree to which B's needs, on B's own metric, are met | Not perception of A's acts | Can occur without perception (invisible support) |
| Giver cost and well-being | What caring costs A, and A's own flourishing | Not dyadic satisfaction | Unmitigated giving harms the giver while the dyad looks healthy |
| Compatibility | Existence of a joint life pattern meeting both people's essential needs and goals | Not understanding | Goal conflict persists under perfect understanding |
| Commitment and stability | Intention and structural reasons to stay; actual persistence | Not love | Stable unhappy relationships exist |
| Trust | Expectation that the other will act benevolently | Not satisfaction | Acts as moderator, mediator and outcome |
| Safety, autonomy and power | Freedom from violence, coercion and intrusion; distribution of dependence | Not "low care" | Harm here is not offset by love or warmth |

## Parental responsibility is not romantic reciprocity

The brief rightly insists that a child is not responsible for providing equivalent care to a parent. Relationship science gives this a formal basis. In **communal relationships**, benefits are given in response to need, not to create debts. In a classic experiment, repaying a benefit *decreased* attraction when a communal relationship was desired and increased it when an exchange relationship was expected ([Clark & Mills 1979](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.37.1.12)) [Established as a distinction]. Communal theory describes parent–child bonds as *asymmetric* communal relationships, in which one party carries more responsibility ([Clark & Mills 2012](https://doi.org/10.4135/9781446249222.n38)) [Theoretical; chapter content not directly verified].

Empirically, care flows mainly downward across the life course. Across ten European countries, parents over 70 remain net givers of time and money ([Albertini, Kohli & Vogel 2007](https://doi.org/10.1177/0958928707081068)). In a five-country study of elders, being able to give enhanced life satisfaction, while being mainly a recipient, and holding strong filial norms, went with *lower* life satisfaction ([Lowenstein, Katz & Gur-Yaish 2007](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-4560.2007.00541.x)) [Moderate/mixed]. Role reversal is linked to later psychopathology, but weakly: self-reported childhood parentification correlates r = .14 with adult psychopathology ([Hooper et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1002/jclp.20807)). The harm appears tied to unfairness and developmental inappropriateness, not to helping as such. Perceived fairness moderated outcomes among adolescents with caregiving duties ([Kuperminc, Jurkovic & Casey 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0014064)), and daily family assistance went with *higher* adolescent happiness through a sense of role fulfilment ([Telzer & Fuligni 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0014728)) [Moderate/mixed].

The framework therefore treats the two relationship types differently:
- **Couples.** Reciprocity is a legitimate standard, and asymmetric dependence predicts worse emotion and outcomes ([Le & Agnew 2001](https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407501183007); [Schoebi, Karney & Bradbury 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0026290)).
- **Parents and children.** The parent's care is not a loan. A child's lack of "repayment" is not a translation failure on the child's side. Expecting repayment can itself be harmful: in Seoul middle-schoolers, parents' *repayment expectation*, but not the child's felt indebtedness, was linked to psychological control and through it to drinking among boys ([Choe, Schoppe-Sullivan & Lee 2026](https://doi.org/10.1177/00332941251415323)) [Preliminary].
