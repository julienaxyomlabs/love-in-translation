# 14. Temporal dynamics and feedback

## What changes, and where the change happens

The [brief](../docs/research-brief.md) asks whether change occurs within individuals, between individuals or through their interaction. The evidence shows all three, on different timescales.

**Within individuals**, three things move. Needs change with development: parent–child conflict affect rises from early to middle adolescence ([Laursen, Coy & Collins 1998](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.1998.00817.x)), and personal-domain autonomy claims grow with age ([Smetana & Asquith 1994](https://doi.org/10.2307/1131311)). Attachment anxiety and avoidance decline across adulthood and with being in a relationship ([Chopik, Edelstein & Grimm 2019](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspp0000167)). Relationship satisfaction follows age- and duration-linked trajectories, falling from 20 to 40 and in the first decade of a relationship ([Bühler, Krauss & Orth 2021](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000342)) [Established, descriptive].

**Between individuals**, selection matters: who stays in a relationship, who reports, and who separates. It creates artefacts such as accelerating terminal declines ([Section 8.9](08-mathematical-representation.md)).

**Through interaction**, mutual cyclical growth links one person's acts to the other's trust and dependence ([Wieselquist et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.77.5.942)). Projected perceptions change one's own later responsiveness ([Lemay & Clark 2008](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.94.4.647)). Rejection expectations elicit rejection ([Downey et al. 1998](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.75.2.545)). Children's behaviour shapes parenting at least as much as the reverse ([Lansford et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1111/jora.12381)) [Moderate/mixed].

## The brief's eight dynamic processes, checked against evidence

| Process | Evidence | Model component |
|---|---|---|
| Needs evolve | Normative adolescent realignment ([Hadiwijaya et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-016-0627-7)); conflict accompanies but does not drive change ([De Goede, Branje & Meeus 2009](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-008-9286-7)) | $`N(t)`$ drift; developmental lag [Our hypothesis] |
| Expectations evolve | Ideals shift toward the partner: early positive perceptions predicted later ideals, not the reverse ([Fletcher, Simpson & Thomas 2000b](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.6.933)) | The need standard itself is endogenous |
| Trust evolves | Trust as outcome, mediator and moderator ([Section 8.9](08-mathematical-representation.md)) | Latent state-space |
| Behavioural strategies adapt | Lower-power partners adapt more ([Righetti et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167215579054)); invisible care earns no thanks ([Visserman et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550618757599)) | Feedback-driven reallocation [Our hypothesis] |
| Communication affects later behaviour | Mostly concurrent within couples; lagged effects weak ([Johnson et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/01461672211016920)) | Weak lagged paths expected |
| Experience shapes later perception | Insecure recipients re-rated earlier care after a disappointing note ([Collins & Feeney 2004](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.87.3.363)); motivated memory ([Lemay & Neal 2013](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0030422)) | Priors updated by outcomes |
| Repeated mismatches accumulate | Not shown. Disillusionment, not emergent conflict, distinguished divorcing couples ([Huston et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.80.2.237)); change is barely predictable ([Joel et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917036117)) | Open question |
| Repair reverses some damage, not all | Apology and reduced perceived intent track forgiveness ([Fehr, Gelfand & Nag 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019993)); acceptance therapy helped about half of distressed couples at 5 years ([Christensen et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018132)); forgiveness without safety signals erodes self-respect ([Luchies et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0017838)) | Asymmetric updating [Theoretical] |

## Where Bayesian updating is justified, and where it is not

Bayesian updating is justified for two inference problems: inferring regard from costly acts ([Section 8.6](08-mathematical-representation.md)) and updating trust after evidence. Laboratory work supports asymmetric updating, in which beliefs about agents who seem bad are more volatile ([Siegel et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-018-0425-1)) [Moderate/mixed, strangers]. No Bayesian or reinforcement-learning trust model has been estimated on couples' everyday data. The closest evidence is a trust game in which people invested more with friends despite equal returns ([Fareri, Chang & Delgado 2015](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4775-14.2015)) [Preliminary].

Dynamical systems with tipping points are not justified as empirical models. Classic love equations were never estimated on data ([Strogatz 1988](https://doi.org/10.1080/0025570X.1988.11977342); [Rinaldi, Della Rossa & Dercole 2010](https://doi.org/10.1142/S021812741002709X)). Statistically rigorous dyadic dynamics, such as coupled oscillators, latent differential equations, threshold autoregression and regime-switching models, have been fitted, but to affect, physiology or coded behaviour, never to care translation ([Butner, Diamond & Hicks 2007](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1475-6811.2007.00164.x); [Butler 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/1088868311411164)).

Time-dependent claims should match the timescale of the data. Daily diaries cannot see minute-scale misreading and repair, and minute-scale coding cannot see developmental drift ([Hamaker 2026](https://doi.org/10.1037/met0000600)) [Established].
