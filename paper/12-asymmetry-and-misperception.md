# 12. Asymmetry, misperception and attribution

## "90 versus 40" is a measurement problem before it is a psychological one

The [brief](../docs/research-brief.md)'s illustration is useful because it separates two sincere reports, but the two numbers are probably not answers to the same question. A giver's "I provided 90% of what was needed" is a judgment of adequacy relative to need. Over-claiming studies measure *share of joint work* ([Ross & Sicoly 1979](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.37.3.322)), and studies of recipients' reactions measure *momentary feeling*. Before calling a gap a translation failure, a study must put both people on the same construct, time frame and scale. On a 0–100 scale with a standard deviation near 20, a 50-point gap would be about 2.5 standard deviations (illustrative arithmetic), several times the mean-level gaps found in parent–child and couple research (g ≈ .2–.5; [Hou et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0000463); [Korelitz & Garber 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-016-0524-0)) [Established]. Gaps that large in real dyads should first be checked for four mundane sources: distress-driven sentiment, different constructs, different scale use, and retrospective reconstruction.

## Mechanisms that pull the two reports apart, with no bad faith required

Five mechanisms can open the gap without either person being insincere:
- **Availability.** Each person remembers their own contributions better, so self-allocated responsibilities sum to more than 100% ([Ross & Sicoly 1979](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.37.3.322)). Unpacking others' contributions reduces over-claiming but made the highest contributors *less* satisfied ([Caruso, Epley & Bazerman 2006](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.91.5.857)) [Established for over-claiming; Moderate/mixed for the intervention cost]. Correcting a giver's inflated view is therefore not automatically benign.
- **Care-type-dependent forecasting.** Givers err upward for costly, material or unrequested care and downward for warm, expressive care ([Section 4](04-evidence-for-the-intuition.md)) [Moderate/mixed; US only].
- **Projection and sentiment.** The receiver's rating reflects their own giving and global sentiment ([Lemay, Clark & Feeney 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.92.5.834); [Hawkins, Carrère & Gottman 2002](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1741-3737.2002.00193.x)).
- **Motivated memory.** Valuing a partner raises remembered responsiveness ([Lemay & Neal 2013](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0030422)). Wives' memories of earlier marriage were negatively biased so that the present seemed better ([Karney & Coombs 2000](https://doi.org/10.1177/01461672002610006)). Couples "remembered" improvements that their contemporaneous scores did not show ([Sprecher 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.76.1.46)) [Moderate/mixed]. Memory should therefore run upward in committed relationships and downward in disengaging ones.
- **Overconfidence.** Intimates overestimate how well they are understood ([Savitsky et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2010.09.005); [Gilovich, Savitsky & Medvec 1998](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.75.2.332)). People also expect *others'* judgments to be self-serving but not their own ([Kruger & Gilovich 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.76.5.743)) [Moderate/mixed].

The classic actor–observer asymmetry is *not* among these mechanisms. Its average effect is near zero ([Malle 2006](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.6.895)) [Established]. What does replicate is a *self-serving valence* pattern, in which people explain their own failures by circumstance and their successes by disposition. A giver who explains his lapses by work pressure and his provision by his character is doing something motivated, not merely perspectival.

## Separating disagreement about facts from disagreement about meaning

| Kind of disagreement | Question | Tool | Key reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Occurrence (facts) | Did the act happen? | Act-level dyadic diaries scored as hits, misses, false alarms; observer coding | [Gable, Reis & Downey 2003](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9280.t01-1-01426); [Coriell & Cohen 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.69.2.289) |
| Meaning | Given that it happened, what did it mean and why was it done? | Matched intent–impact ratings of the *same* acts; standardized stimuli with stranger benchmarks; attribution measures | [Gaelick, Bodenhausen & Wyer 1985](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.49.5.1246); [Noller 1980](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0077716); [Fincham & Bradbury 1992](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.62.3.457) |
| Need model | Did the giver know what was needed? | Giver predicts the receiver's needs; truth-and-bias tracking | [West & Kenny 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0022936) |
| Scale use | Do the two use the scale differently? | Anchoring vignettes; directional-bias terms; response surface analysis instead of difference scores | [King et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1017/S000305540400108X); [Schönbrodt, Humberg & Nestler 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/per.2169) |
| Memory | Is the gap created by recall? | Compare aggregated daily reports with end-of-period global reports | [Shiffman, Stone & Hufford 2008](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.clinpsy.3.022806.091415) |

Anchoring vignettes carry a conceptual trap. They assume everyone perceives a described act at the same level, which is precisely what the translation hypothesis denies. Formal tests of their assumptions have also failed in health data ([Bago d'Uva et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.3368/jhr.46.4.875)). The workable compromise is non-care vignettes to calibrate scale use, with care-act vignettes treated as *outcome* data, so that differences in how people value standardized acts become a measure of translation [Assumption].

## A decomposition of the gap [Our hypothesis]

A giver's global adequacy rating and a receiver's global experienced-care rating can be decomposed by successive substitution:
1. **Memory.** Replace each global rating with that person's aggregated daily ratings.
2. **Scale use.** Recalibrate both with vignettes.
3. **Occurrence.** Restrict to acts both people agree occurred.
4. **Meaning.** Compare the giver's intended value with the receiver's experienced value for matched acts.
5. **Need model.** Compare the giver's predicted need with the receiver's reported need.

The residual after these steps is what remains to be called translation in the strict sense. Because successive-substitution decompositions depend on order, the contributions should be averaged over orderings (a Shapley-value approach) [Assumption]. This is a measurement proposal, not a finding. The hypothesis loses if most of a global gap disappears at steps 1–3.

Parent–child report discrepancies are not mere error; they can carry information ([De Los Reyes et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-clinpsy-050212-185617)). Tested with polynomial interactions rather than difference scores, discrepancies did predict adolescent problems ([Laird & De Los Reyes 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10802-012-9659-y)) [Moderate/mixed]. Agreement about parenting is *lower* in more individualistic societies such as the US, although the mean gap between parents' and adolescents' reports does not differ with individualism ([Hou et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0000463)). A Korean conference abstract on very old parents and their children reported parents describing more support and less conflict than their children did ([Han et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/geroni/igaa057.1119)) [Preliminary].
