# 16. What we cannot quantify

The framework is honest only if it names its blind spots:
- **Love itself.** Love has no measurement independent of self-report and behaviour. Its components can be measured, but their sum is not "the amount of love." In self-report, love shares a higher-order factor with satisfaction ([Fletcher, Simpson & Thomas 2000](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167200265007)).
- **Intentions.** These are only inferable. Every accuracy estimate uses another person's report as its criterion, which is not ground truth ([Perilloux & Kurzban 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614555727)). "Love intended" is therefore permanently a latent construct with self-reported proxies.
- **The full meaning of an act.** Construal can be indexed (attributions, inferred motives, perceived regard) but not exhausted. The same act carries histories, symbols and family codes that no scale captures, and Korean concepts such as *jeong* remain largely philosophical constructs without validated measures ([Chung & Oh 2022](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-94747-7_1)).
- **Counterfactual care.** "What she would have felt had he been home more" is not observable for one family. Only randomized within-family designs approximate it, and only for small, ethical manipulations.
- **A dyad's own translation matrix.** How much money "counts" as time for this daughter is not estimable from one family's data ([Section 8.11](08-mathematical-representation.md)). Relationship-specific misperception cannot be identified within a single couple.
- **Normative weights.** How much autonomy should count against closeness, where the safety threshold lies, and what a child owes a parent are value questions. Data can inform them but not settle them.
- **Tipping points.** Whether trust is bistable is currently untestable at acceptable ethical cost ([Section 8.9](08-mathematical-representation.md)).
- **Individual prediction from group data.** Group-level results describe individual couples only for ergodic processes. Within-person relations vary far more than group estimates suggest, by up to four times in one analysis ([Fisher, Medaglia & Jeronimus 2018](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1711978115); [Molenaar 2004](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15366359mea0204_1)).
