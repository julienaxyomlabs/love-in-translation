# 4. Evidence supporting the original intuition

The intuition has real empirical support, and the strongest support is more specific than the original wording. The evidence is organized here by the link in the chain it supports.

## Care is lost between act and perception, measurably and systematically

The occurrence of care itself is contested. Supporters and recipients agreed only at κ = .39 about which supportive behaviours had taken place ([Coriell & Cohen 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.69.2.289)). Romantic sacrifices were as likely to be missed as detected ([Visserman et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550618757599)). In a dyadic daily-diary study, the enactor's own beliefs about a behaviour and the perceiver's interpretation *jointly* predicted daily mood and satisfaction. For negative behaviours, the enactor's perspective had effects of its own ([Gable, Reis & Downey 2003](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9280.t01-1-01426)) [Moderate/mixed].

The closest test of whether *love specifically* gets lost is small but striking. In 29 couples, communicators' intended feelings were compared with recipients' perceived feelings. Negative feelings were decoded accurately and positive feelings inaccurately, and men read the *absence* of love as hostility ([Gaelick, Bodenhausen & Wyer 1985](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.49.5.1246)) [Moderate/mixed; small N]. In the classic "talk table" studies, distressed and non-distressed spouses reportedly did not differ in the *intended* impact of their messages, but distressed spouses' messages were *received* as more negative than intended ([Gottman et al. 1976](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.34.1.14)). That result rests on a secondary summary ([Gottman Institute](https://www.gottman.com/blog/the-research-behavior-exchange-theory-and-marital-decision-making/)) [Preliminary].

Intimates also overestimate how well they communicate. Spouses understood each other's ambiguous phrases no better than strangers did, yet were far more confident that their spouse had understood ([Savitsky et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2010.09.005)). Low-adjustment spouses were equally confident in their correct and incorrect decodings ([Noller & Venardos 1986](https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407586031003)) [Moderate/mixed]. This overconfidence is exactly the cognitive condition under which a giver can sincerely say "I gave you everything" while the receiver sincerely disagrees.

## Givers and receivers systematically disagree about the same care

Several independent literatures document **systematic giver–receiver asymmetries**:
- Members of couples and work groups over-claim their share of joint work. In couples, their self-allocated responsibilities for household activities summed to more than 100% ([Ross & Sicoly 1979](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.37.3.322)). Over-claiming grows with group size ([Schroeder, Caruso & Epley 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/xap0000080)).
- In 4,055 Dutch parent–adult-child dyads, both generations over-reported help given and under-reported help received ([Mandemakers & Dykstra 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1741-3737.2008.00496.x)) [Moderate to Established].
- Fathers rate themselves as more affectionate than their sons rate them ([Floyd & Morman 2005](https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407505049323); [Morman & Floyd 2002](https://doi.org/10.1080/10570310209374746)).
- Hong Kong parents reported more sacrifice than their adolescents perceived, and the father–adolescent gap was larger than the mother–adolescent gap ([Leung & Shek 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12187-015-9332-4)) [Preliminary].
- Across 313 studies, parents described their parenting more positively than adolescents did (g = .24). Children's reports of acceptance fall further below parents' as they age, reaching g ≈ −.50 by age 14 and over for both mothers and fathers ([Hou et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0000463); [Korelitz & Garber 2016](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-016-0524-0)) [Established].

The *direction* of the giver's error depends on the kind of care, which supports a refined version of the intuition [Moderate/mixed]:
- Givers expected pricier gifts to earn more appreciation, but recipients' appreciation was unrelated to price ([Flynn & Adams 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2008.11.003)).
- Givers assumed unrequested gifts would be valued as much as requested ones, and recipients disagreed ([Gino & Flynn 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2011.03.015)).
- For warm, expressive acts the error reverses. Writers of gratitude letters, givers of compliments, people performing random kindness and people reaching out with support all *underestimated* how positively recipients would respond ([Kumar & Epley 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797618772506); [Zhao & Epley 2021](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspa0000277); [Boothby & Bohns 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167220949003); [Kumar & Epley 2023](https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0001271); [Dungan, Munguia Gomez & Epley 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976221082942)).
- The proposed mechanism is itself a translation mechanism: **givers judge care by its competence or cost, receivers by its warmth** ([Dungan, Munguia Gomez & Epley 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/09567976221082942); [Galak, Givi & Williams 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/0963721416656937)).

## Form and fit change what the same amount of care does

The best evidence that "how" matters beyond "how much" comes from support research:
- Both under- and over-provision of support predicted declining marital satisfaction ([Brock & Lawrence 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0015402)).
- Emotional support offered after an emotional disclosure predicted perceived sensitivity, while mismatched support lowered satisfaction ([Cutrona et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0893-3200.21.4.754)).
- Visible and invisible support helped only when responsive ([Maisel & Gable 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02388.x)).
- Visible support could backfire by signalling inefficacy ([Bolger & Amarel 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.92.3.458)).
- Partners' availability, *non-interference* and encouragement predicted exploration, while intrusive "help" undermined it ([Feeney & Thrush 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016961)) [Moderate/mixed].

The meaning a receiver assigns also changes the effect:
- Sacrifices seen as aimed at the receiver's own welfare produced gratitude, while those seen as self- or relationship-focused did not ([Visserman et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000344)).
- At high levels, adolescents read both behavioural and psychological control as signalling that they *matter less* to their parents ([Kakihara & Tilton-Weaver 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2009.01364.x)) [Preliminary].
- Across 35 studies, perceptions of a *partner's* relationship maintenance predicted outcomes more strongly than one's *own* enactment ([Ogolsky & Bowers 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407512463338)).
- Across 72 samples, perceptions of the partner's dyadic coping predicted satisfaction more strongly than perceptions of one's own ([Falconier et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2015.07.002)) [Established, associational].
- Biased models of a partner's preferences shaped how people expressed affection, and accurate models went with satisfaction ([Coy & Rodriguez 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/jmft.12655)) [Preliminary].

## Culture and generation change which signals count as care

The same act can carry different meanings across groups:
- In a cross-national study, Asian college students received *more* frequent parental support than German or US students but were *less satisfied* with it ([Fingerman et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/0192513X14541444)) [Moderate/mixed]. This is among the most direct quantitative illustrations of "provided ≠ received".
- In 562 Korean emerging adults, helicopter parenting was associated with *more* depressive symptoms through pressure from parental career expectations and with *fewer* through parent–child affection ([Lee & Kang 2018](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10826-018-1193-2)). These are opposite paths from one behaviour, depending on construal [Moderate/mixed].
- Asian and Asian American participants were more hesitant to seek explicit support because of relationship concerns, and benefited more from implicit or unsolicited support ([Taylor et al. 2004](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.87.3.354); [Taylor et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.01987.x); [Mojaverian & Kim 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167212465319)).
- Chinese immigrant and European American mothers valued warmth equally but expressed it through partly different channels, nurturance and instrumental support versus direct affection ([Cheah et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0039855)) [Preliminary].

Several gaps predict outcomes:
- Chinese American adolescents whose *ideal* parental warmth exceeded the warmth they perceived had more adjustment problems. For second-generation youth, appreciating parental devotion and sacrifice buffered this link ([Wu & Chao 2005](https://doi.org/10.1080/01650250500147444); [Wu & Chao 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021063)).
- Parent–child acculturation gaps correlate with intergenerational conflict (r = .23), which correlates with offspring mental health (r = −.20) ([Lui 2015](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0038449)).
- In one longitudinal sample, the pathway from such a gap to unsupportive parenting, alienation and depressive symptoms was stronger in father–adolescent dyads ([Kim et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0029169)) [Moderate/mixed].

## Stress and change create failures of capacity and updating

Husbands under chronic stress were less accurate about their wives' support needs across diary days. Even when they noticed a need, they were less likely to act on it on days they were coping with their own stress ([Neff, Nguyen & Williamson 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167220974490)) [Moderate/mixed]. Under higher stress, global satisfaction tracked daily relationship events more closely ([Neff & Karney 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0015663)).

Developmental change creates the need for updating. Perceived parental support dips from early to middle adolescence and recovers later for girls, while parental power declines ([De Goede, Branje & Meeus 2009](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-008-9286-7)). From ages 12 to 16, adolescents move away from parental authority, and relationships improve from 16 to 20 ([Hadiwijaya et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10964-016-0627-7)) [Moderate/mixed]. A parent whose model of the child does not keep pace has, in effect, a translation problem.

## Unrecognized givers pay a price, so translation failure runs both ways

The hypothesis is usually framed from the receiver's side, but the data show a cost to the *giver* as well. Givers whose sacrifices went unnoticed were less satisfied on those days ([Visserman et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550618757599)). Being thanked strengthened the benefactor's own sense of connection the next day ([Algoe, Gable & Maisel 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1475-6811.2010.01273.x)) [Moderate/mixed]. A father who says "I gave you everything" may be expressing exactly this: the cost of care that was given but never credited.
