# 15. Failure cases and counterexamples

The framework was stress-tested against the [brief](../docs/research-brief.md)'s eight cases. Each case asks which component of the framework handles it, whether the case breaks the framework, and what remains unexplained.

| Case | Handled? | Component that handles it | What it still cannot do |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Perfect understanding, incompatible lives | **Yes, by construction** | Compatibility as feasibility ($`\mathcal{F} = \varnothing`$), separate from translation | Measure essential requirements $`G`$ reliably |
| B. Manipulative responsiveness | **Partly** | Motive inference; giver motives measured separately; repeated, behaviour-anchored assessment | Detect manufactured responsiveness from the receiver's reports alone |
| C. Love and abuse | **Only by excluding it from the care calculus** | Safety admissibility constraint (unsafe arrangements excluded, not outranked); perception treated as a biased, capturable sensor | Explain abuse as translation (it is not) |
| D. Parental love with autonomy violation | **Yes** | Autonomy as a separate outcome; construal moderated by domain legitimacy | Say how much cultural meaning should attenuate harm in a given family |
| E. Good intentions, harmful outcomes | **Yes** | Context-dependent sign of care; mis-targeting; asymmetric loss | Specify the context function in advance |
| F. Unsustainable needs | **Yes** | Giver sustainability threshold within $`\mathcal{F}`$; giver well-being as a separate outcome | Measure need load against capacity |
| G. Different but successful expressions | **Yes** | Level ($`a_1`$) dominant; breadth; shared codes | Predict which couples build shared codes |
| H. Stability without strong romantic love | **Yes, by separating love, commitment and stability** | Love components versus commitment versus outcomes | Settle whether quality is comparable across marriage types |

**Case A: perfect understanding, incompatible lives.** In couples paged six times a day, closeness and well-being were highest when an activity served *both* partners' goals. Pursuing only the partner's goals was costly ([Gere et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jrp.2011.06.010)). Each partner's report of goal conflict was associated with lower relationship quality and well-being for both ([Gere & Schimmack 2013](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10902-011-9314-2)) [Moderate/mixed]. Interdependence theory treats the correspondence of interests as a structural property of situations, distinct from the information partners hold ([Rusbult & Van Lange 2003](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.54.101601.145059)). The framework handles this case because compatibility is a separate construct: understanding locates a point in $`\mathcal{F}`$ if one exists but cannot create one. It is also a direct counterexample to "the problem is often translation", since here the problem is no translation at all. No study has crossed verified high understanding with measured goal incompatibility, so the case is handled in theory and untested in data.

**Case B: manipulative responsiveness.** Self-interested actors can manufacture warmth. Narcissism's most exploitative facets were the most attractive at first sight ([Back, Schmukle & Egloff 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0016338)), and self-enhancers' early appeal faded within weeks ([Paulhus 1998](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.74.5.1197)). Ingratiation reliably increases liking unless it is seen as transparent ([Gordon 1996](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.71.1.54)). Emotion-regulation knowledge amplified the link from Machiavellianism to interpersonal deviance ([Côté et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611416251)). Affection made contingent on compliance predicted poorer relationship quality through frustrated autonomy ([Kanat-Maymon et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/jopy.12171)) [Moderate/mixed].

Two caveats keep this case honest. **No study has measured perceived partner responsiveness inside manipulative or abusive relationships.** And "love bombing" has essentially no peer-reviewed evidence base. A PubMed title and abstract search run for this review in November 2026 returned no records, and a popular summary likewise notes that little peer-reviewed research exists ([Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/love-bombing)) [Preliminary at best]. Receivers are not passive either. People discount regard they suspect is inauthentic ([Lemay & Clark 2008b](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.95.2.420)).

The framework handles the case partly: responsiveness is a *means*, not a motive, so giver motives (communal versus self-oriented goals) must be measured independently of the receiver's perceptions. The case breaks any model that validates against perceived responsiveness alone.

**Case C: love and abuse.** Love, commitment and abuse coexist routinely. In a meta-synthesis of 282 women's accounts, early violence was discounted "for the sake of romantic commitment" ([Kearney 2001](https://doi.org/10.1002/nur.1029)). Among highly committed people, tolerance for a partner's aggression rose once it began; commitment was measured, not manipulated ([Arriaga, Capezza & Daly 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000035)). People in aggressive relationships overestimated how unhappy a break-up would make them ([Arriaga et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550613480822)). Staying is predicted by poor economic alternatives and investments ([Rusbult & Martz 1995](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167295216002)) [Moderate/mixed].

Coercive control is linked to PTSD symptoms at r = .32 ([Lohmann et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/15248380231162972)). Nonviolent coercive control produces fear comparable to violent intimate terrorism ([Conroy & Crowley 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/08862605211005149)). Lifetime physical or sexual partner violence affects about 27% of ever-partnered women aged 15–49 worldwide ([Sardinha et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(21)02664-7)) [Established].

The framework handles this case **only by placing it outside the care calculus**. A model that scores love perceived or received would rate some abusive relationships as high-functioning, because commitment, dependence and cultural scripts *capture* the perception channel. Framing abuse as a "translation failure" would also symmetrize responsibility and pathologize the victim's perception. The case falsifies any version of the hypothesis in which perceived love is the criterion of relationship health.

**Case D: parental love that violates autonomy.** Sincere love combined with autonomy violation is the *modal* case, not the exception. Dependency-oriented psychological control is linked to parental separation anxiety, and achievement-oriented control to parental perfectionism ([Soenens, Vansteenkiste & Luyten 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-6494.2009.00614.x)). Mothers in four cultures, including Korean immigrant mothers, gave care-oriented reasons for control ([Cho et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1037/dev0001136)). Conditional regard achieves compliance at an emotional cost ([Assor, Roth & Deci 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.0022-3506.2004.00256.x)) [Moderate/mixed].

Cultural normativeness *reduces, sometimes; it does not remove* the harm. Physical discipline predicted problems regardless of its normativeness ([Lansford et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2005.00847.x)). Spanking effects did not differ by ethnicity in a US national sample of 11,044 ([Gershoff et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2011.01732.x)). Children's own perceived normativeness buffered some effects in China ([Liu & Wang 2018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chiabu.2018.09.009)). The "tiger parenting" profile predicted worse, not better, outcomes for Chinese American youth ([Kim et al. 2013b](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0030612)) [Moderate/mixed].

The framework handles the case by treating autonomy as a separate outcome, with control over the *personal* domain as the key moderator. In the US and Japan, perceived parental control over personal issues was associated with internalizing symptoms, while control over conventional issues was not ([Hasebe, Nucci & Nucci 2004](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2004.00708.x)). This is where translation-type appraisal has its best support, but appraisal *attenuates* harm; it does not reverse it.

**Case E: good intentions, harmful outcomes.** Forgiveness, positive expectations and benevolent attributions helped in healthy marriages but predicted *worse* outcomes in troubled ones ([McNulty & Fincham 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0024572); [McNulty, O'Mara & Karney 2008](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.94.4.631)). A tendency to forgive predicted continued partner aggression ([McNulty 2011](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167211407077)). Accommodating a family member's anxiety maintains the problem: a parent-only programme that *reduces* accommodation was noninferior to child cognitive-behavioural therapy in a randomized trial ([Lebowitz et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2019.02.014)) [Moderate; strongest causal anchor]. Protective buffering of ill partners went with lower satisfaction ([Hagedoorn et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-6133.19.3.274)).

The framework handles this case: care has a **context-dependent sign**, so a fixed positive weight on "care expressed" is misspecified. Its limitation is that the context function is not specified in advance.

**Case F: unsustainable needs.** Communal motivation of every kind predicted *relationship* well-being, but only non-unmitigated forms predicted the giver's *personal* well-being ([Le et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000133)). High unmitigated communion even predicted a *slower* decline in satisfaction over seven years ([Horne, Impett & Johnson 2020](https://doi.org/10.1037/fam0000620)), so the dyad looks healthy while the giver pays. Strained caregivers faced higher mortality risk ([Schulz & Beach 1999](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.282.23.2215)), although caregiving overall was not harmful in population studies ([Roth, Fredman & Haley 2015](https://doi.org/10.1093/geront/gnu177)). Excessive reassurance seeking correlates with interpersonal rejection at r = .14 ([Starr & Davila 2008](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0013866)) [Moderate to Established]. The framework handles the case through the giver-sustainability threshold within $`\mathcal{F}`$ and by measuring giver well-being separately. The case falsifies any dyad-level index used alone.

**Case G: different but successful expressions.** Spouses express love through partly different behaviours across 13 years of marriage. Wives showed it by enacting fewer negative behaviours, and husbands by initiating sex, shared leisure and joint housework ([Schoenfeld, Bredow & Huston 2012](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167212450739)). Couples build private idioms whose number tracks love and closeness ([Bell, Buerkel-Rothfuss & Gore 1987](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2958.1987.tb00121.x)) and a "shared reality" they defend under threat ([Rossignac-Milon et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000266)). Breadth of loving behaviour predicted quality better than any primary channel ([Flicker, Sancier-Barbosa & Impett 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/jmft.70078)) [Moderate/mixed]. The framework handles this case: if level and breadth dominate mismatch, different channels are compatible with health. Similarity in emotions and values does correlate with quality in some samples ([Gonzaga, Campos & Bradbury 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.93.1.34)), so "difference is fine" holds for channels more than for values.

**Case H: stability without strong romantic love.** Moral and structural commitment are distinct from personal, love-based commitment ([Johnson, Caughlin & Huston 1999](https://doi.org/10.2307/353891)). Stable but unhappy marriages exist and carry costs to happiness, self-esteem and health ([Hawkins & Booth 2005](https://doi.org/10.1353/sof.2005.0103)). About half of divorces in a US national sample ended low-distress marriages ([Amato & Hohmann-Marriott 2007](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1741-3737.2007.00396.x)). Small selected samples of arranged marriages report satisfaction comparable to love marriages ([Regan, Lakhanpal & Anguiano 2012](https://doi.org/10.2466/21.02.07.PR0.110.3.915-924)), while larger probability samples in Chengdu and Nepal favour self-chosen marriage ([Xu & Whyte 1990](https://doi.org/10.2307/352936); [Allendorf & Ghimire 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ssresearch.2012.09.002)) [Moderate/mixed]. The framework handles the case because love, commitment and stability are separate constructs. Whether companionship without strong romance yields equal quality remains unresolved, and spouse choice is confounded with education and modernity.
