# 19. Limitations and unresolved scientific questions

**This synthesis inherits the limits of its sources.** Most samples are from WEIRD societies. The pooled machine-learning analysis drew only on Western samples ([Joel et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917036117)), and the giver–receiver forecasting asymmetries have no located non-US replication. Many findings rest on abstracts rather than full texts, and some on two short diary studies (sacrifice detection) or single samples (over-provision over five years). A primary-source audit of the 24 most consequential claims found eight of the twenty main ones resting wholly or partly on abstracts or press accounts ([`../audit/`](../audit/README.md)). Several often-cited results could be verified only through secondary sources, notably the talk-table intent–impact gap, and are tagged Preliminary. Korean psychological evidence on the *meaning* of care is mostly cross-sectional, often from 2010 or earlier, and sometimes from one research group. It predates large changes in fathers' time use and leave-taking.

**The central comparative question has never been tested directly.** No study has measured intended care, enacted care, detection, construal, need fulfilment and outcomes in the same dyads over time. None has compared variance explained by translation indices with variance explained by amount-of-love indices in one longitudinal sample. No study has randomized channel match at fixed amount. No study has measured perceived responsiveness inside coercive relationships, so the "capture" claim rests on adjacent evidence. No study has followed parents' models of their children's needs over time.

**The formal model has its own limits.** Its original components are untested: the two routes, the visibility asymmetry, developmental lag, the safety admissibility rule and compatibility as feasibility. The naïve utility calculus has never been estimated on family data. Several parameters, including the translation matrix and the trust interpretation function, are not identifiable at feasible cost. The decision to exclude unsafe arrangements, and where to set the safety threshold, are partly normative.

**Measurement may not support the distinctions the theory needs.** Self-reported perceived regard, need fulfilment and satisfaction share a dominant general sentiment factor ([Kim et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0342451)). Distinctions the model draws may therefore be hard to recover from self-report alone, which is why the proposed design leans on giver logs, observers and randomization.

**Five scientific questions remain open:**
1. Is over-provision worse than under-provision, and on what timescale?
2. Do the giver–receiver forecasting asymmetries hold in long-term relationships and outside the US?
3. Does appraisal-focused change, such as reframing provision as care, help as much as behaviour change? The only relevant randomized evidence, from anxiety accommodation, changed behaviour ([Lebowitz et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2019.02.014)).
4. Do daughters and sons read paternal provision differently?
5. Do the large changes in Korean family norms since 2014 alter how provision is construed?
