# 6. Comparison of the strongest existing psychological theories

No existing theory covers the whole chain from intention to outcome. Several cover individual links well. The table compares them on the question this paper asks: what each explains about care moving between people, and what it misses.

## Responsiveness, need fulfilment, empathy and interpretation versus love languages

The [brief](../docs/research-brief.md) asks whether responsiveness, need fulfilment, empathy and interpretation are stronger explanatory constructs than love languages, without assuming that they are. On evidence quality the answer is clear for two of them and qualified for the other two.

**Responsiveness and need fulfilment** have meta-analytic, longitudinal, multi-country and physiological support that love languages lack. Their weaknesses are shared: heavy reliance on self-report, overlap with global sentiment, and reverse causation from satisfaction to perception ([Kim et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0342451); [Joel et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917036117)) [Moderate to Established]. Both contain the defensible kernel of the love-language idea, that people feel loved when partners give what they value, without its unsupported claim that one channel carries special weight.

**Empathy, in the sense of empathic accuracy, is a weak explanatory construct** for relationship quality (r = .134; [Sened et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1037/fam0000320)) [Established]. Its value lies mainly in its boundary conditions: reading a partner's distress matters more than reading their affection, and accuracy can hurt when what is accurately perceived is threatening.

**Interpretation**, meaning attributions, inferred motives and construal, has moderate longitudinal support and is the most direct mechanism for "the same act, different meaning." Its causal role, however, is bidirectional ([Fincham, Harold & Gano-Phillips 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0893-3200.14.2.267)).

The comparison therefore favours a framework built on responsiveness, need fulfilment and interpretation, with commitment and interdependence as the structural backbone. It also warns that responsiveness and need fulfilment as currently *measured* are partly the same global sentiment that the outcome measures capture.

## Which theory covers which link

| Link in the chain | Best-supported theory | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| Love → intention | Communal motivation; compassionate love; investment model | Good for motivation; intentions rarely measured |
| Intention → enactment | Vulnerability–stress–adaptation; capacity ([Neff, Nguyen & Williamson 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167220974490)) | Partial |
| Enactment → detection | Quasi-signal detection ([Gable, Reis & Downey 2003](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9280.t01-1-01426)); invisible support | Partial (descriptive) |
| Detection → construal | Attribution, attachment, sentiment override, motive inference | Good for negative acts; thin for positive and material acts |
| Construal → perceived regard | Perceived responsiveness; truth and bias | Good, but projection confounds it |
| Enactment → need fulfilment | Support matching; need fulfilment; income effects | Good in specific domains |
| Outcomes → feedback | Mutual cyclical growth; gratitude; child effects | Partial |
| Structure and constraints | Interdependence; power; coercive control; self-determination (autonomy) | Good conceptually; rarely integrated with care |
