# 3. Critical interdisciplinary literature review

This section reviews each literature before any modelling. It separates what studies *measured* from what their authors *inferred*. Sections 4 and 5 then sort the findings into evidence for and against the hypothesis.

## 3.1 Perceived responsiveness predicts well-being, but perceivers partly project it

**Perceived partner responsiveness** (PPR) is the sense that a partner understands, validates and cares for you. It is the central construct of the intimacy process model ([Laurenceau, Barrett & Pietromonaco 1998](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.74.5.1238)) and has a validated self-report scale ([Reis et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1002/9781119102991.ch57)). In the largest pooled analysis of relationship predictors, 43 longitudinal dyadic datasets from 29 laboratories (11,196 couples), PPR was among the top relationship-specific predictors of relationship quality. It ranked behind perceived partner commitment, appreciation, sexual satisfaction and perceived partner satisfaction ([Joel et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917036117)) [Established, for prediction].

The health findings are smaller than often implied. In the US MIDUS cohort, PPR was measured with only three items (the partner cares, understands, appreciates). It predicted change in eudaimonic well-being over a decade (B = .057), not hedonic well-being ([Selcuk et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/jomf.12272)). It explained about 2% of the variance in later cortisol slopes ([Slatcher, Selcuk & Ong 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797615575022)). Its indirect path to mortality did not survive covariates ([Stanton et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1097/PSY.0000000000000618)) [Moderate/mixed]. PPR also predicted well-being more weakly in Japan than in the US ([Tasfiliz et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/fam0000378)). A Chinese version had to be *constructed* rather than translated because some original items were judged culturally inappropriate ([Zhou et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40359-022-00865-x)) [Preliminary].

The finding that matters most for the hypothesis concerns *where* perceived responsiveness comes from. In dyadic marriage studies, a spouse's **own** responsiveness predicted their perception of the partner's responsiveness *more strongly* than the partner's self-reported responsiveness did. Experimentally making one's own past support feel harder to recall lowered perceived partner responsiveness ([Lemay, Clark & Feeney 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.92.5.834); [Lemay & Clark 2008](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.94.4.647)) [Moderate/mixed]. In 102 Swiss dating couples sampled four times a day, the partner's enacted responsiveness did reach the perceiver's PPR, but the perceiver's own enacted responsiveness predicted it too ([Debrot et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028666)). A modern scale built from 246 candidate items found that respondents distinguish two valence-based dimensions, *responsiveness* and *insensitivity*, rather than the three theoretical components. In 161 couples it detected a genuine partner-behaviour signal beyond projection ([Crasta et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1037/pas0000986)) [Moderate/mixed]. A review concludes that feeling understood is only modestly related to actually being understood ([Reis, Lemay & Finkenauer 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/spc3.12308)). Perceived responsiveness is therefore partly a reading of the partner and partly an echo of the perceiver.

## 3.2 The intimacy process holds within moments but is untested as a causal chain

Diary studies support the model's core claim. In 96 married couples followed for 42 days, one's own disclosure and the partner's disclosure each predicted same-day intimacy, with perceived responsiveness as a *partial* mediator ([Laurenceau, Barrett & Rovine 2005](https://doi.org/10.1037/0893-3200.19.2.314)). Observer-coded responses to a partner's disclosures predicted relationship well-being and break-up two months later. Responses to *good* news were more predictive than responses to bad news ([Gable, Gonzaga & Strachman 2006](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.91.5.904)). Enthusiastic responses to shared good news increased trust in experiments with strangers ([Reis et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018344)) [Moderate/mixed].

What has not been shown is that changing a partner's responsive behaviour in an established couple *causes* later intimacy through perceived responsiveness. No randomized test of that sequence was located, and most tests rely on the same person reporting disclosure, responsiveness and intimacy in the same moment.

## 3.3 Received support and perceived support are different things

Support research supplies the clearest quantitative evidence that care given and care received come apart:
- Across 23 studies, *received* support (reported enacted behaviours) and *perceived* support (felt availability) correlated only **r = .35** ([Haber et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10464-007-9100-9)) [Established for that measure pairing].
- When students and their supporters independently reported which supportive behaviours had occurred after an exam, agreement was **κ = .39** ([Coriell & Cohen 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.69.2.289)) [Moderate/mixed].
- In designs where many perceivers rate many supporters, the largest variance component in perceived supportiveness was the unique *perceiver × supporter* pairing, not the supporter's general supportiveness ([Lakey et al. 1996](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.70.6.1270)).
- In 288 Dutch families, support perceptions were determined **more by the perceiver than by the partner** ([Branje, van Aken & van Lieshout 2002](https://doi.org/10.1037/0893-3200.16.3.351)) [Moderate/mixed].

Relational regulation theory reinterprets the pair-specific share as compatibility of taste in ordinary conversation, not as successful transmission of stress-related support ([Lakey & Orehek 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023477)) [Theoretical, with supporting studies].

**Invisible support** is the cleanest dissociation between benefit and perception:
- Among couples facing the bar exam, support the provider reported but the recipient did not notice predicted *better* adjustment ([Bolger, Zuckerman & Kessler 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.6.953)).
- In three experiments (N = 257), invisible support reduced emotional reactivity, while visible support was ineffective or harmful because it communicated inefficacy ([Bolger & Amarel 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.92.3.458)).
- Visible support raised same-day satisfaction *and* anxiety. Invisible support produced no same-day cost but higher satisfaction the next day ([Girme et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/fam0000453)).
- Visible emotional support helped highly distressed recipients and cost non-distressed ones ([Girme, Overall & Simpson 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167213497802)) [Moderate/mixed].

Two qualifications matter. Both visible and invisible support were beneficial **only when responsive** ([Maisel & Gable 2009](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02388.x)). And support that helps recipients understand their situation and feel capable predicted mood and motivation more strongly than perceived responsiveness did ([Zee, Bolger & Higgins 2020](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000235)) [Moderate/mixed].

**Fit matters, but the evidence on excess is mixed:**
- Under the optimal-matching account, emotional support following emotional disclosure predicted perceived sensitivity, while mismatched support lowered satisfaction via lower sensitivity. Matching informational support did not ([Cutrona et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0893-3200.21.4.754)).
- In 103 newlywed couples followed for five years, both under-provision and over-provision of support predicted declining satisfaction, and **over-provision was the greater risk** ([Brock & Lawrence 2009](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0015402)).
- A daily study found the reverse: under-provision harmed, while over-provision had minimal effect ([Bar-Kalifa & Rafaeli 2013](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0034905)) [Moderate/mixed].
- Daily support receipt raises closeness *and* negative mood at once ([Gleason et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.94.5.824)).
- Gender differences lie in the *timing* of support, not its amount ([Neff & Karney 2005](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.88.1.79)).

## 3.4 Sacrifice, gratitude and good news show how recognition changes the value of care

A meta-analysis of 82 datasets (N = 32,053) separated four facets of sacrifice ([Righetti et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000297)) [Established, associational]:
- *Willingness* to sacrifice was positively associated with both partners' well-being (.09 < r < .27).
- Actual *behavioural* sacrifice was slightly negatively associated with the giver's personal well-being (r = −.07).
- *Satisfaction* with sacrifice was positively associated with well-being (.11 < r < .43).
- *Costs* of sacrifice were negatively associated (−.10 to −.26).

Recognition is where translation becomes concrete. In two daily-experience studies (N = 426), partners' sacrifices were **as likely to be missed as detected**, and about half the sacrifices people "saw" had not been reported by the partner. Perceived sacrifices, accurate or not, produced gratitude. Missed sacrifices produced none, and on days they sacrificed, unrecognized givers were less satisfied ([Visserman et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550618757599)) [Moderate/mixed]. The *motive* inferred also matters. Gratitude followed when a sacrifice was seen as aimed at promoting one's welfare, not when it was seen as relationship- or self-focused ([Visserman et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/emo0000344)). Avoidance-motivated sacrifice was particularly corrosive ([Impett, Gable & Peplau 2005](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.89.3.327)).

Gratitude for thoughtful benefits predicted next-day connection for *both* recipient and benefactor ([Algoe, Gable & Maisel 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1475-6811.2010.01273.x)). How thanks is expressed matters: other-praising thanks was perceived as more responsive and made benefactors feel loved ([Algoe, Kurtz & Hilaire 2016](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550616651681)). Appreciating a partner predicted staying together ([Gordon et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028723)) [Moderate/mixed]. Gratitude is plausibly both a channel through which care is translated and a marker of love already present, and diary designs cannot exclude reverse causation.

## 3.5 Accuracy, bias and attribution: people are accurate and biased at once

Romantic perception is surprisingly accurate *and* biased. A meta-analysis distinguished **tracking accuracy** (knowing *when* and *on what* a partner is higher or lower), r = .47 across 98 studies, from **mean-level bias**, r = .09 across 48 studies. The two were statistically independent. For judgments about the partner's love and communication, the signed bias was slightly *negative*: people slightly underestimate their partner's love ([Fletcher & Kerr 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019792)) [Established]. In ratings made every 30 seconds while reviewing recorded conflict discussions, perceivers underestimated their partner's regard yet tracked its changes well ([Overall, Fletcher & Kenny 2012](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167211432764)).

Assumed similarity, or projection, was "considerably stronger" than accuracy in 238 couples ([Kenny & Acitelli 2001](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.80.3.439)) [Moderate/mixed]. The truth-and-bias model formalizes this. A judgment is pulled by a truth force toward the criterion, by projection toward one's own standing, and by directional bias toward one end of the scale ([West & Kenny 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0022936)) [Established as a model]. Cronbach long ago showed that raw "agreement" scores confound several of these components ([Cronbach 1955](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0044919)).

Accurately reading a partner's inner states, known as **empathic accuracy**, relates only weakly to satisfaction: r = .134 across 21 studies (N = 2,739). The link holds for reading negative emotions (r = .171) but not positive ones ([Sened et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.1037/fam0000320)) [Established]. Accuracy can even hurt. Perceiving a partner's relationship-threatening thoughts accurately predicted declines in closeness ([Simpson, Oriña & Ickes 2003](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.85.5.881)). Newlyweds' *subjective* sense of understanding predicted well-being, while actual knowledge of the partner did not ([Pollmann & Finkenauer 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167209342754)) [Moderate/mixed].

**Sentiment override** is the strongest rival to translation as a cause. Global sentiment colours how specific behaviours are rated, relative to objective coders, for positive and low-intensity negative affect but not for high-intensity hostility ([Hawkins, Carrère & Gottman 2002](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1741-3737.2002.00193.x)). Expectations held *before* an interaction mediated the satisfaction–affect link ([Fincham et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0893-3200.9.1.3)) [Moderate/mixed]. Low-adjustment spouses decoded their *own* spouse's nonverbal messages worse than other spouses did, but decoded strangers no worse. The author read this as communication problems being a consequence, not a cause, of unhappiness ([Noller 1981](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.41.2.272)) [Moderate/mixed].

**Attributions** carry meaning from act to outcome. Non-benign attributions for a partner's negative behaviour predicted lower satisfaction a year later, controlling for initial satisfaction ([Fincham & Bradbury 1993](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.64.3.442)). The effect runs in both directions over time ([Fincham, Harold & Gano-Phillips 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0893-3200.14.2.267)), although initial attributions predicted later satisfaction more than the reverse ([Karney & Bradbury 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.78.2.295)) [Moderate/mixed]. The classic actor–observer asymmetry, by contrast, is essentially zero on average (d = −0.016 to 0.095 across 173 studies), so it cannot carry a translation theory ([Malle 2006](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.6.895)) [Established].

## 3.6 Needs, attachment, commitment and trust: the deep structure beneath perception

**Need fulfilment** is among the best-supported constructs:
- Relationship-specific fulfilment of autonomy, competence and relatedness predicts attachment security *with that partner* ([La Guardia et al. 2000](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.79.3.367)).
- Both partners' need fulfilment uniquely predicts one's own relationship functioning ([Patrick et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.92.3.434)).
- Needs met in the current relationship relative to alternatives distinguished people who stayed from those who left ([Drigotas & Rusbult 1992](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.62.1.62)) [Moderate to Established].

Two aggregation results bear directly on the mathematics. People with **balanced** need satisfaction reported higher well-being than people with the same total but more uneven profiles, which is a non-compensatory component ([Sheldon & Niemiec 2006](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.91.2.331)). Across 123 countries, specific needs related to well-being largely independently of whether other needs were met ([Tay & Diener 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023779)) [Moderate to Established]. Need satisfaction scales show measurement equivalence across Belgium, China, the US and Peru, and their effects were not moderated by how strongly people valued each need ([Chen et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-014-9450-1)) [Established within student samples]. This hints that individual differences may lie more in *how* needs are best met than in *which* needs matter.

**Attachment** shapes perception, but within limits. In the key experiment, partners copied experimenter-written notes, so everyone in a condition received identical messages ([Collins & Feeney 2004](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.87.3.363)) [Established for this bounded effect]:
- Clearly supportive notes were appraised the same by secure and insecure recipients, with a large condition effect (β = .66) and no attachment effect.
- Only *ambiguous*, low-support notes were appraised more negatively by avoidant (β = .41, sr² = .06) and anxious (β = .40, sr² = .06) recipients.
- Insecure recipients of disappointing notes then *re-rated an earlier interaction* as less supportive.

Insecurity predicts lower relationship quality in meta-analyses ([Li & Chan 2012](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.1842); [Candel & Turliuc 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.paid.2019.04.037)). Attachment is dimensional rather than categorical ([Fraley et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspp0000027)). It is moderately stable and changes with age and relationship experience: being in a relationship predicted declining anxiety and avoidance ([Chopik, Edelstein & Grimm 2019](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspp0000167)) [Moderate to Established].

**Commitment and trust** are the strongest structural predictors. Satisfaction (r = .65), investments (r = .53) and quality of alternatives (r = −.43) predict commitment across 202 samples ([Tran, Judge & Kashima 2019](https://doi.org/10.1111/pere.12268)). Commitment, love, inclusion of other in the self and dependence are **among the strongest predictors of non-marital break-up** across 137 studies ([Le et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1475-6811.2010.01285.x)) [Established]. Trust grows through *mutual cyclical growth*: commitment produces pro-relationship acts, the partner *perceives* them, perceived acts build trust, and trust increases willingness to depend. Acts that depart from self-interest are especially diagnostic ([Wieselquist et al. 1999](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.77.5.942)) [Moderate to Established]. A partner's behavioural inclination to maintain the relationship predicted divorce over 11 years independently of satisfaction, and risk followed the *weaker* partner ([Schoebi, Karney & Bradbury 2012](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0026290)).

## 3.7 Communication matters less, and less causally, than its reputation

Communication and satisfaction correlate robustly, but within couples the dominant pattern is **concurrent**. Three longitudinal studies in the US, Switzerland and Germany found that couples were more satisfied than usual at times of less negative communication than usual. Lagged effects in either direction were weak, and positive communication was rarely associated with satisfaction within couples ([Johnson et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/01461672211016920)). In low-income newlyweds, communication→satisfaction and satisfaction→communication paths both existed but "neither pathway was particularly robust" ([Lavner, Karney & Bradbury 2016](https://doi.org/10.1111/jomf.12301)) [Established to Moderate].

Demand/withdraw (r = .36 with outcomes across 74 studies; [Schrodt, Witt & Shimkowski 2014](https://doi.org/10.1080/03637751.2013.813632)) and observed hostility ([Woodin 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0023791)) are reliable correlates of distress. Forgiveness tracks the offender's perceived intent (r = −.49), empathy (r = .51) and apology (r = .42) ([Fehr, Gelfand & Nag 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0019993)) [Established, associational].

Interventions are the most demanding test of any "fix the translation" account, and they disappoint:
- Relationship education produces modest short-term effects (d ≈ .3 for quality) in mostly middle-class samples ([Hawkins et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0012584)).
- These effects vanish for premarital programmes once unpublished studies are included ([Fawcett et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1741-3729.2010.00598.x)).
- They were null or slightly negative in a randomized trial with more than 4,000 low-income couples ([Wood et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1111/jomf.12094)).
- In a randomized trial with 174 couples, two skills programmes and a one-session awareness intervention *with no skills training* each cut three-year dissolution from 24% to 11%. The three did not differ ([Rogge et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0034209)).
- Acceptance-based couple therapy outperformed behaviour-change therapy only through two years. By five years, clinically significant improvement was 50.0% versus 45.9% ([Christensen et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018132)) [Moderate to Established].

A decade review concluded that negative communication is hard to change, that changing it does not reliably raise satisfaction, and that processes adaptive for middle-class couples may work differently in lower-income couples ([Karney & Bradbury 2020](https://doi.org/10.1111/jomf.12635)).

## 3.8 Family and culture: perceived acceptance travels, specific signals do not

The family literature is reviewed in depth in Sections 10 and 13. Three findings frame it.

First, **perceived** parental acceptance correlates with children's self-reported adjustment in every country studied. The correlation is r = .51 in the theory group's meta-analysis of 43 studies ([Khaleque & Rohner 2002](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1741-3737.2002.00054.x)). What was *measured* was the correlation between two questionnaires completed by the same respondent, which shared method inflates. What was *inferred* was a universal effect of acceptance on adjustment. Prospective nine-country data put it as small but nearly universal ([Putnick et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12366)) [Established, associational]. The predictor is the child's perception, not the parent's intent. Child-driven effects are pervasive, so low perceived warmth can be a consequence of child difficulties as well as a cause ([Rothenberg et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0954579419001214)) [Moderate/mixed].

Second, **money matters causally, but conditionally**. Natural experiments and fixed-effects studies show household income improves children's behavioural, educational and health outcomes, mainly at low incomes ([Cooper & Stewart 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12187-020-09782-0); [Costello et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.290.15.2023)). The largest US randomized cash trial found no developmental or maternal mental-health benefits by age four ([Noble et al. 2025, NBER](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33844); [Duncan et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapediatrics.2025.1612)) [Moderate/mixed].

Third, the same act can carry different meanings in different groups, but meaning **attenuates rather than reverses** links with harm. Physical discipline was less harmful where perceived as normative, yet harmful regardless ([Lansford et al. 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8624.2005.00847.x)). Parenting–outcome associations show "more similarities than differences" across 428 studies ([Pinquart & Kauser 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/cdp0000149)) [Established].

## 3.9 Love languages: the popular framework fails its own central claims

The Five Love Languages framework makes three claims: each person has a primary language, there are five, and partners are happier when they speak each other's language. A 2024 review concluded the evidence "does not provide strong empirical support" for any of the three ([Impett, Park & Muise 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/09637214231217663)) [Moderate/mixed]. The direct tests are consistent:
- In a **pre-registered** study of 499 cohabiting US adults, fewer than half had an identifiable primary language. Satisfaction with the partner's expression of one's primary language predicted relationship quality no better than satisfaction with other loving behaviours ([Flicker, Sancier-Barbosa & Impett 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/jmft.70078)).
- In 696 adults, Chapman's hypothesis was "not supported" ([Flicker & Sancier-Barbosa 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/jmft.12747)).
- Bottom-up factor analyses found that 7–10 love languages fit better than five ([Flicker et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/jmft.70072)).
- The five-factor structure failed in 250 Indonesian couples, and language compatibility did not predict marital satisfaction ([Surijah, Prasetyaningsih & Supriyadi 2021](https://doi.org/10.15575/psy.v7i2.6634)).
- Alignment showed "limited evidence" in 67 couples ([Bunt & Hazelwood 2017](https://doi.org/10.1111/pere.12182)) [Moderate/mixed overall].

The main positive result shows why the framework appears to work. In 100 couples, a summed mismatch between one partner's preferred ways of receiving love and the other's expression predicted lower satisfaction (β ≈ −.36 to −.40) ([Mostova, Stolarski & Matthews 2022](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0269429)) [Preliminary]. That index rises whenever the partner gives *less* of what one wants, so it confounds "wrong kind" with "not enough". It never controlled for the overall amount of love expressed. Biased models of a partner's preferences do shape how affection is expressed, and accurate models go with satisfaction ([Coy & Rodriguez 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/jmft.12655)) [Preliminary]. Both studies fit perceived responsiveness ("my partner gives me what I value") at least as well as love-language matching.

The best-powered analogue of "matching" tests partner-preference matching in a registered report with 10,358 people in 43 countries. The idiosyncratic matching effect fell from β = .19 to β = .11 once a normative "everyone likes this" component was controlled, and trait-specific matching averaged β ≈ .04 ([Eastwick et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspp0000524)) [Established]. The defensible kernel of the love-language idea is that people feel loved when partners give what they value. That kernel is the definition of responsiveness and need fulfilment, and the framework's distinctive claim, that one channel carries special weight, has not been shown. The "balanced diet" metaphor fits the data better than the "language" metaphor ([Impett, Park & Muise 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/09637214231217663)).

## 3.10 Safety, coercion, autonomy and power: where perception fails as a sensor

Sections 15 and 8.10 treat this literature in detail. The central findings are these.
- Commitment shifts personal standards toward tolerance once aggression begins, and only among the highly committed. Study 2 was longitudinal and Study 3 experimental, the latter showing more tolerance for acts imagined from one's partner than from a stranger ([Arriaga, Capezza & Daly 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000035)).
- Third parties judged men who hit their wives out of jealousy to love their wives as much as men who did not ([Puente & Cohen 2003](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167202250912)) [Moderate/mixed].
- Psychological control of children (guilt induction, love withdrawal, intrusion) is associated with ill-being at r = .26, or r = .20 controlling for autonomy support, across 238 studies and 126,423 participants. The association applies across world regions, national individualism and cultural hierarchy ([Bradshaw et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0001389)) [Established, associational].
- Lower-power partners sacrifice more ([Righetti et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167215579054)).
- Power weakens the link from gratitude to perceived responsiveness ([Jin, Zhu & Wang 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-71994-z)) [Moderate/Preliminary].

| Primary love language and matching are unsupported | Moderate/mixed (null) | [Flicker, Sancier-Barbosa & Impett 2025](https://doi.org/10.1111/jmft.70078); [Impett, Park & Muise 2024](https://doi.org/10.1177/09637214231217663) | Cross-sectional, US-dominated |
| Congruence terms add little beyond levels | Moderate/mixed | [Yang et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hrmr.2008.07.014) (ΔR² ≈ .008); [Dyrenforth et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0020385) (< .5%) | Work and personality domains, not care |
| Direction of giver error depends on care type | Moderate/mixed | [Flynn & Adams 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jesp.2008.11.003); [Kumar & Epley 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797618772506) | US only; strangers and one-off acts |
| Parent–child report gaps are real but small | Established | [Hou et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0000463) (g ≈ .24) | Parenting reports, not care adequacy |
| Income causally improves children's outcomes in poverty | Moderate to Established | [Cooper & Stewart 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12187-020-09782-0) | Null cash RCT at age four; mostly US |
| Psychological control relates to ill-being across cultures | Established (associational) | [Bradshaw et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1037/amp0001389) | Mostly cross-sectional, youth-reported |
| Perceived love is not a valid safety indicator | Moderate/mixed | [Arriaga, Capezza & Daly 2016](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspi0000035); [Puente & Cohen 2003](https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167202250912) | Small US samples; no study of perceived responsiveness inside abusive relationships |
| Trust or satisfaction is bistable, with tipping points | Theoretical; no empirical evidence | [Rinaldi & Della Rossa 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30898194/) | Models never estimated on relationship data |
