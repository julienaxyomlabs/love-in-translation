# Evidence audit and study protocol

*Version 0.3.0, November 2026. The paper audited here is [v0.2.0](https://github.com/julienaxyomlabs/love-in-translation/tree/v0.2.0); its text and equations are unchanged in v0.3.0.*

**The audited evidence supports a narrow yes: love can be lost in translation, but in a narrower form than the v0.2.0 paper states, and only some parts of the theory can be told apart from simpler explanations.** Givers' and receivers' reports of the same care often disagree, unregistered sacrifices bring no same-day gratitude, and in laboratory analogues the same support helps or hurts depending on how it is read. No audited study shows care that demonstrably occurred being lost, because every "occurrence" criterion in these studies is another self-report. An independent audit of the 20 most consequential empirical claims (C01–C20) and four supplementary claims (S1–S4) found no fabricated citation, and every quoted coefficient matched its source. Yet **four claims are overstated as used, and each anchors one of the model's equations**: stress "blocking" enactment (component 1), over-provision being worse than under-provision on a commensurate scale (component 5), invisible support proving a route that bypasses perception (component 6), and felt love rising as safety falls (component 10). A fifth error reverses a sign: for judgments of a partner's love, the meta-analytic bias is slightly negative (r = −.11), not positive (component 3). **Eight of the twenty claims rest wholly or partly on abstracts or press accounts**, including the two that carry the route split and the safety case. The frozen mathematics needs no change; the evidence attached to it does. For the next phase, the decisive question is identifiability. **Detection, construal, inference of regard from cost, and channel match versus intensity can be separated from rival explanations with realistic designs. The split between the substantive and symbolic routes cannot be identified by randomisation alone.** At the level of outcomes, the symbolic route is observationally identical to "perceived support alone", and compatibility and intention have no valid measures. The protocol in Part C is a design document only: no participant data have been collected and no one has been recruited.

**Table S1. What the next phase can and cannot distinguish.**

| Part of the theory (v0.2.0 equation) | Strongest rival | Realistic design that can separate them | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Expressed ≠ perceived: detection (2) | Misses are a criterion shift driven by the receiver's trust or sentiment | Paired evening diaries with signal-detection coding; an observer-coded laboratory criterion; randomised prompts as a known signal | Separable descriptively, and causally for prompted acts |
| Construal: tracking, projection, level bias (3) | Perception is generated by the perceiver (strong form of "perceived support alone") | Within-person truth-and-bias models with giver and observer criteria | Separable, but the "truth" criterion is another report |
| Regard inferred from cost (4) | Regard is read from the act alone | Between-subjects vignette experiment with unrelated adults | Separable causally; transfer to real families untested |
| Fit versus level (5) | Total support received | Multilevel response surfaces on commensurate need and supply; micro-randomised match-versus-intensity prompts | Associational for the surface; causal for same-evening outcomes; statistical power is the binding risk |
| Symbolic route, $`\beta_{\text{sym}}`$ (6) | Perceived support alone; need fulfilment | Within-person, lagged incremental prediction; tests of discriminant validity | Identical to "perceived support alone" at the outcome level; separable from need fulfilment only if the two constructs prove distinct |
| Substantive route, $`\beta_{\text{sub}}`$ (6) | Perceived support alone; need fulfilment | Giver-logged or randomised enactment, with mediation and sensitivity analysis | Not identified by randomisation; at best bounded under an untestable no-confounding assumption |
| Relationship-specific misperception (§8.11) | Perceiver trait (trust, attachment) | Four-member family round robin | Separable descriptively; confounded with relationship-specific trust unless that is measured |
| Trust dynamics and the giver's lag (7–8) | Stable trust; projection | Long dyadic series with dynamic structural equation models | Direction partly estimable; reading function, bistability and learning rate practically unidentifiable |
| Intended ≠ expressed (1) | Self-presentation in intention reports | Giver diaries with capacity proxies | Weak: intention has no validated measure |
| Received ≠ compatibility (9) | — | Goal-conflict proxies | Not testable with realistic designs |
| Safety (10) and autonomy | Not rivals: a constraint and an outcome | Private screens; separate outcome models | Admissibility is normative; data can show only whether care predicts outcomes below a safety threshold |

**How to read these documents.** Parts A, B and C and the list of corrections are filed separately (see [Contents](#contents)), and each stands alone. Sources carry the verification depth the audit reached: **full text** (the article body was read), **abstract** (publisher, PubMed, OpenAlex or Crossref abstract only), **secondary** (press or practitioner summaries only) or **metadata** (bibliographic record only). Grades use the paper's own scale (Established, Moderate or mixed, Preliminary, Theoretical, Our hypothesis, Assumption). Details the research notes mark UNVERIFIED or PRIOR KNOWLEDGE are labelled as such. Repository locations are `file:line` at the [v0.2.0 tag](https://github.com/julienaxyomlabs/love-in-translation/tree/v0.2.0). The audit covers the cited studies, not the wider literature.

## `claims-audit.csv`

Generated by [`tools/build_audit_ledger.py`](../tools/build_audit_ledger.py) from the `jsonl` block at the end of each audit note (08–11). CI fails if it is stale. Do not edit it by hand: correct the note, then run `make audit`.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `claim_id` | `C01`–`C20` (the twenty most consequential claims) or `S1`–`S4` (supplementary claims the repository flags as resting on secondary or unverified sources) |
| `short_claim` | The claim as the paper uses it |
| `source` | The primary study |
| `doi` | Its DOI, where one exists |
| `verification_depth` | `full text`, `abstract`, `secondary` (press or practitioner summaries only) or `inaccessible` |
| `sections_read` | Which parts of the source were read |
| `design`, `population` | Study design; sample and setting |
| `measured` | What the study actually measured |
| `key_result` | The result as reported in the source |
| `supports_interpretation` | `supported`, `qualified`, `overstated` or `cannot determine` |
| `evidence_grade` | The auditors' grade on the paper's scale. A judgement, not a consensus rating |
| `paper_locations` | `file:line` locations in v0.2.0 that use the claim |
| `recommended_change` | The correction the auditors recommend |
| `notes` | Caveats |
