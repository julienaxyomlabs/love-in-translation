# Text corrections the audit implies for the v0.2.0 paper

This page lists the changes to the text of *Can Love Be Lost in Translation?* (version 0.2.0) that follow from a primary-source audit, carried out in November 2026, of its 20 most consequential empirical claims (C01–C20) and four supplementary claims (S1–S4).

**Status: applied in v0.3.0.** Locations are `file:line` at the [v0.2.0 tag](https://github.com/julienaxyomlabs/love-in-translation/tree/v0.2.0), the version audited. How each recommendation was applied, with the corrected wording, is logged in [`CORRECTIONS.md`](../CORRECTIONS.md).

**The mathematics needs no change.** The audit found no error in equations (1)–(10). Every correction concerns:
- the evidence attached to an equation;
- the justification given for an equation's form;
- the wording, numbers or grades around them.

**Source depths.** Each source is marked with the depth the audit reached: full text, abstract, or secondary (press or practitioner summaries).

**One location is new to this synthesis.** One location (`paper/08-mathematical-representation.md:316`) was found while preparing this report and was not recorded in the audit notes. It is marked †.

**Generated files.** `evidence/claims.jsonl` is generated from the paper, so it updates once the chapters change.

**A corrections-log entry.** Because the v0.2.0 corrections log states that "No empirical finding was re-graded", the next version should add a `CORRECTIONS.md` entry that records the re-graded findings below, with their verification depth.

**What the corrections change, taken together.** The corrected paper says less than v0.2.0 on both sides of its own argument, and most of the loss falls on the ten-component model.

- **The case against the naive thesis weakens.** The evidence that amount matters more than translation is weaker than stated, because neither the Le et al. study nor the Flicker et al. study measured love *received*.
- **The case for the model's distinctive parts weakens more.** Four components lose evidence:
  - the capacity bound;
  - the asymmetric fit;
  - a route that bypasses regard;
  - the empirical motive for the safety constraint.

What survives intact is detection and the symbolic mechanism. The safety admissibility rule also survives: it now rests openly on normative and design grounds, and the corrected evidence that committed receivers' tolerance drifts is a cleaner argument for deciding safety from behavioural indicators. The next version can therefore state its central claim more exactly. **Care is often not registered as given, and how it is read changes what it does. Whether care also works through paths that bypass regard, and whether fit matters beyond amount, are open empirical questions that the Part C design can partly settle.**
