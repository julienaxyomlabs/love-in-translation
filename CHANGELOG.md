# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented here. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and the project uses [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [0.3.0] - 2026-11-05

An evidence audit, competing predictions and a study protocol, asking which parts of the theory data can tell apart from simpler explanations. The equations are unchanged. The paper text was corrected wherever the audit found an overstatement, a wrong number or a misattribution, and every change is logged in [CORRECTIONS.md](CORRECTIONS.md).

### Added

- `audit/evidence-audit.md` (Part A). A primary-source audit of the 20 most consequential empirical claims and four supplementary claims. For each claim it records what was measured, the design, population and limitations, whether the source supports the paper's use, and the verification depth reached:
  - 12 claims were read in full text;
  - 10 from abstracts only;
  - 2 from press or blog accounts.
- `audit/claims-audit.csv`. The audit as data, one row per claim, generated by `tools/build_audit_ledger.py` (`make audit`). CI and two tests keep it in step with the notes.
- `audit/corrections.md`. 75 recommended text changes in three tiers, with grade changes.
- `protocol/competing-predictions.md` (Part B). Predictions of the substantive and symbolic routes against four rivals:
  - total support received;
  - perceived support alone;
  - need fulfilment;
  - existing trust or attachment.

  It also states which observations favour each model and where realistic designs cannot separate them.
- `protocol/study-protocol.md` (Part C). A dyadic longitudinal design for a Korean case study, with a measurement phase, instruments and their Korean validation status, intervals, identification assumptions, competing models, power strategy, confounders, falsification criteria and safety procedures. Design only: no data collected, no one recruited.
- `evidence/notes/08`–`12`, the audit and protocol notes. Links in them are pinned to v0.2.0, the version audited.
- Contribution rule 8: the equations are frozen at v0.2.0 and change only to fix a substantive error.

### Fixed

- `tools/check_dois.py` retries a lookup that fails on a network error. On Python 3.9 a read timeout used to escape and crash the check.

### Changed (paper text, after the audit)

- The verdicts were reworded:
  - intended ≠ expressed is *plausible*;
  - expressed ≠ perceived is *supported as disagreement between giver and receiver reports*;
  - perceived ≠ received is *supported in one direction, in a laboratory analogue; mixed in the other*;
  - "translation, not amount" is *open in both directions*, because the evidence cited against it does not compare amount with translation;
  - the closing answer no longer claims that love is at least as often weakened at its source as lost in translation.
- Four overstated claims were narrowed:
  - the original invisible-support diary result did not replicate, so a route that bypasses perceived regard is now *Our hypothesis*: unproven, not refuted (C04);
  - over-provision is not shown to be worse than under-provision, and that measure is not commensurate (C06);
  - stress dampens enactment rather than blocking it (C14);
  - the safety evidence concerns tolerance standards, not love ratings (C19).
- The bias in judgments of a partner's love is now described as slightly negative (C09, r = −.11). The equations did not change; δ is a free parameter.
- Eight findings were re-graded. Sample sizes were corrected for Visserman et al. and Bolger & Amarel, and an age result was reattributed from Hou et al. to Korelitz & Garber.
- The Korean validation status of three instruments was corrected. Sommet et al. (2023) is now cited for interactions only. The micro-randomised trial defines a channel match from the previous evening's need report.
- The captions for the substantive route in Figure 1 and in the model-at-a-glance figure now match its definition in §8.8.
- The README and the citation abstract state that the model is a theoretical, empirically unvalidated framework, and that the DOI check is bibliographic only.
- Three references were added: Shrout et al. 2010, Kim et al. 2022 and Yeom & Lee 2024.
- The claims ledger now holds 305 graded claims, up from 280. Corrections split several tagged spans and added tags to claims that had none.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-04

Corrections prompted by an external review; each is documented with earlier wording, sources and verification depth in [CORRECTIONS.md](CORRECTIONS.md).

### Fixed

- Response-surface interpretation: $`a_1`$ is the slope along the line of congruence at the centring point only, and a negative $`a_4`$ is one part of a joint congruence pattern, not a mismatch penalty by itself (C1).
- Safety is now an admissibility constraint that excludes unsafe arrangements and recommends nothing when none is safe; the lexicographic ranking it replaces still picked among unsafe options (C3, ADR-0009 supersedes ADR-0005).

### Changed

- The substantive and symbolic routes are described as pathways of effect for any act, not as categories of acts (C2).
- The README separates what the tests and DOI checks show from empirical validation, which has not been done (C4).
- `constraints.classify()` reports `"unsafe"` when only unsafe arrangements meet both people's requirements; `JointPattern` gains a `safe` field.

### Added

- "Model at a glance" in the README and chapter 8: the ten equations, what each says, its method and evidence status, every variable's unit and domain, and identifiability limits; figure `paper/figures/model-at-a-glance.png`.
- `fit.stationary_point()`, `fit.principal_axes()`, `fit.congruence_screen()` and $`a_5`$; tests reproduce the worked examples in Edwards (2007) and Schönbrodt, Humberg & Nestler (2018).
- `constraints.admissible()` and `constraints.choose()`.
- `CORRECTIONS.md`.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-04

### Added

- Paper in 22 chapters (`paper/`), with GitHub-native math, Figure 1 as SVG plus editable Mermaid source, and navigation between chapters.
- `love_translation` reference implementation (standard library only): similarity pitfalls, response surface analysis, quasi-signal detection, truth-and-bias decomposition, cost-based motive inference, trust dynamics, developmental lag, safety gate and feasibility.
- `love-translation` command-line interface: `cosine-demo`, `trust`, `lag`, `infer`, `claims`.
- 36 tests executing the paper's formal claims, plus repository-integrity tests.
- Evidence ledger `evidence/claims.jsonl` (276 graded claims), `evidence/references.bib`, `evidence/doi-verification.csv` and the seven literature reviews in `evidence/notes/`.
- Tooling: claims extraction, Markdown lint, DOI verification, BibTeX build, single-file paper build.
- CI on Python 3.9–3.13, monthly citation re-check, issue forms and architecture decision records.
